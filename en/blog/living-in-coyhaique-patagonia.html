Living in Coyhaique: quality of life in Patagonia

Seasonal climate, services, connectivity and lifestyle in the capital of Aysén.

Updated: 15-11-20265 min read
Photo: Jorge Pacheco / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 2.0

Coyhaique is a small city ringed by hills, with the Simpson River alongside and a national reserve a few kilometres from the centre. Living in Coyhaique means snowy winters, mild summers and Patagonia right on your doorstep. It also means planning ahead: the city is almost 1,700 km from Santiago, and the weather sets the pace.

This guide brings together verified facts for anyone thinking of moving to, or spending long stretches in, the capital of the Aysén Region: population, healthcare, universities, climate, air quality, connections and the moment the city is going through.

How many people live in Coyhaique: 2024 Census

According to Chile's 2024 Census (INE), the municipality of Coyhaique has 57,823 inhabitants, practically the same as in 2017 (57,818). The Aysén Region has 100,745 inhabitants, and Coyhaique is home to about 57% of them, according to Emol.

The foreign-born population of the municipality is 2,505 people, 91.5% more than in 2017, according to Chile's National Migration Service.

Watch out for figures in circulation: INE's projection for 2024 (about 62,960 inhabitants) is not the census count.

Healthcare and universities in Coyhaique

Healthcare

The Coyhaique Regional Hospital, at Dr. Jorge Ibar 68, is the only high-complexity hospital in the region and belongs to the Aysén Health Service.

Higher education

  • Universidad de Aysén: a state university whose first academic year was opened by President Bachelet. It has faced falling enrolment: about 660 students in 2024, according to Ex-Ante.
  • Universidad Austral de Chile, Patagonia Campus: in Coyhaique since 2004.
  • INACAP also has a campus in Coyhaique.

Climate and air quality in Coyhaique

Summers are mild and cool; winters are cold, with frequent frosts and snow. According to Chile's Meteorological Office (Teniente Vidal station), normal annual rainfall is 1,023.1 mm. The station's all-time records are −19.2 °C (22 June 2002) and 35.7 °C (4 February 2019).

We do not have the official monthly temperature normals. As a rough reference, an unofficial source using 1998–2020 data gives an average of about 14.6 °C in January and about 3 °C in July. Winter can be tough: in August 2026 a cold snap with heavy snow led the municipality to set up an emergency shelter village.

Winter air quality

There is one fact worth knowing before you decide. Coyhaique is a saturated zone for fine particulate matter (PM2.5), mainly because of wood-burning heating in winter. The city has an Atmospheric Decontamination Plan, which is being updated according to the Ministry of the Environment. There were 51 critical episodes in 2021 and 64 in 2022.

It is not a reason for alarm, but it is a reason to make informed choices: living on a plot outside the urban area and using efficient heating helps.

Connections: airport, highway and fibre

New Balmaceda Airport terminal

Coyhaique opened the new terminal at Balmaceda Airport on 5 October 2026, about 50 minutes by car from the city. The building grew from 2,400 m² to 12,391 m², has 5 jet bridges (up from 2) and capacity for more than 1 million passengers a year. Emol reported that the terminal is rated for international travel. Work is under way with Brazil and Argentina to bring in flights during the 2027 summer, although these routes are not confirmed. From Santiago, LATAM and Sky fly direct in about 2 h 20 min, and flights from Puerto Montt take around 1 h 22 min. More details in our guide on how to get to Coyhaique.

Carretera Austral

According to the Ministry of Public Works (MOP, April 2026), the Carretera Austral is about 1,058 km long, of which 614.5 km are paved (58%). The "Ruta Austral: Soberanía que conecta" plan sets aside about CLP 800 billion for 2026–2030, including 150.4 km of permanent paving. Read more in our Carretera Austral road trip guide.

Internet

The "Última Milla" fibre-optic project will reach around 8,000 homes in Aysén, according to Infraestructura Pública.

A growing city: retail and land

A July 2026 Emol report on Coyhaique mentions announcements by Falabella, Cencosud and SMU; SMU bought a large plot of about 40,000 m² on the road to Balmaceda for about UF 185,000. According to Colliers, commercial land costs between 12 and 25 UF/m², and large plots in expansion areas between 4.5 and 8 UF/m². Brokers estimate land prices have risen between 25% and 60% in three years.

The same report shows the other side: it describes the "subdivision of the whole perimeter" of the city, putting pressure on water, energy and roads, and the Chamber of Commerce reports falling sales for more than a year. So if you plan to buy, check access, water and electricity carefully. Our guide to buying land in Chilean Patagonia explains which documents to ask for.

Frequently asked questions

What is the population of Coyhaique?

According to the 2024 Census, the municipality has 57,823 inhabitants.

How cold does Coyhaique get?

Winters are cold, with frequent frosts and snow. As an unofficial reference, the July average is around 3 °C and the January average around 14.6 °C.

Is there good healthcare?

The Coyhaique Regional Hospital is the only high-complexity hospital in the Aysén Region.

Is Coyhaique polluted?

In winter, yes. The city is a saturated zone for PM2.5 because of wood-burning heating, and its decontamination plan is being updated. Living outside the urban area and using efficient heating helps.
